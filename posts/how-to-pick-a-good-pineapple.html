How to Pick a Good Pineapple: 22 Years of Experience in Southern China ​

Twenty-two years of growing up in southern China, and what they taught me about picking the best pineapple on the shelf.

Pineapple basics ​

A pineapple is not a climacteric fruit: it has no respiratory climacteric. Which means its ripeness is fixed the moment it is cut from the plant — the longer it sits, the worse it tastes.

The "eyes" of a pineapple are the pollinated part of the plant. Digging them out is a nuisance, but they are an inseparable part of a pineapple's life.

The tingling in your mouth comes mostly from oxalic acid and protease in the flesh, so in theory heating the flesh stops the tingling (but who would do that?).

How to pick a good pineapple ​

Sometimes you come across a pineapple with no scent but an orange colour. To reduce confusing, the priority is 1 > 2 > 3 > 4 > 5.

1. Variety matters most ​

Variety is the most important thing about a pineapple. In southern China there are more than 10 varieties; in Southeast Asia there are hundreds of commercial ones. This guide is about picking the best pineapple on the shelf — but which shelf you pick from matters just as much.

PS: I have only been in Europe for a month, but Denmark does not seem to care much about fruit varieties. The pineapples on the shelf are basically MD2 from the Philippines.

2. Freshness decides everything that follows ​

As I said earlier, "a pineapple is not a climacteric fruit, it has no respiratory climacteric", so freshness is the first thing to judge. After a pineapple has been sitting for a while, its colour slowly turns yellow — which distorts people's judgement.

The leaves tell you how fresh the whole pineapple is. Once they are wilted, do not buy it — no matter how fragrant it is, no matter how yellow the colour is.

IF the pineapple is the most yellow on the shelf, but the leaves show that it has been sitting there for a long time.

PS: the pineapples on the Føtex shelf have all had their leaves removed — you can see how sneaky they are!

3. Scent is the key indicator of ripeness ​

Ripe fruit releases scent to attract animals, which eat it and spread the seeds. Pineapple is one of them.

When pineapple gets 100% ripe, the scent is as strong as the flavoring in pineapple candy.

Unfortunately, because of geography, it is hard to find a fully ripe pineapple in the north, so the ones on the shelf are mild.

If you are lucky enough to meet a pineapple that smells like industrial flavouring, just buy it!

Some pineapples stay green even when they are ripe, so scent is the indicator of ripeness.

4. Colour ​

A pineapple ripens from the bottom upwards and from the eyes outwards, so the eyes and the base are usually the yellowest and it gets greener towards the crown. That is why we judge ripeness by the base and the eyes: pale yellow is average, yellow is good, orange is excellent.

Although the base is not broadly yellow, the colour around the eyes is evenly distributed and is yellow and orange — these two are still very good choices.

This one has a large yellow area at the base, but it is all pale yellow, and the colour around the eyes is green — so it is not very ripe.

5. Luck ​

Even with a good eye, meeting a great pineapple still takes luck.

That is the best pineapple I had ever met: fresh leaves, smells like industrial flavouring, a large orange area. But I have never had one that good again. Back then it sat on the shelf and nobody paid it any attention — which is exactly what the saying describes:

"Swift horses are common enough; what is rare is the one who can recognise them."

"Pearls are everywhere but not the same as the eyes."
